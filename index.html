How this comparison works

For each input with sufficient history, the screen learns a 1–10-session lead and direction using older training data. It compares the strongest 24 individual candidates, all pairs among them, and triples among the strongest eight. Equal-weight combinations are selected on a later validation period, then checked on a separate newest holdout. It does not test every possible subset. The score is the percentage of eligible stock capsule events correctly indicated minus the percentage of eligible sessions with an incorrect alert. The held-out hit rate and false alerts are shown separately.

Inputs are standardized using training data only. A fixed threshold indicates rises or falls, and an alert is counted as a hit only if it matches the stock capsule’s confirmation session and direction. A numbered match is shown only in the held-out portion of the current window. Selected and unselected predictors may be correlated or include the stock itself. Delayed/revised feeds, reused holdouts when changing the window, and extensive searches can create spurious results. This is not proof, a point-in-time backtest, a calibrated probability, or a trading recommendation.

How earlier candidates are screened

Inputs are selected using the current sector and industry, then compared with the stock across 1–10 trading-session leads. The lead and direction are chosen on the first 70% of the displayed range; candidates appear in the line ranking only if the remaining 30% has at least 15 valid pairs, keeps the same correlation direction, and has an absolute correlation of at least 0.03. This is a permissive display filter, not a significance test. Indexed lines stay on their actual dates and use pre-window volatility scaling; they are not predictive paths.

Capsule rows require at least one selected-period stock-event match after a source confirmation. The worker chooses combinations on a later validation period, then hides cards with no match in the final holdout. Because display now uses holdout results too, that holdout is no longer untouched for display selection. Low samples, false alerts, multiple testing, timing errors, shared causes and revised or delayed data can produce chance matches. A match does not establish causation or profitability.

Sources, privacy & limits

Stocks and other market series use unofficial Yahoo endpoints via public relays. Economic data comes from FRED. Regional weather uses Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0; free noncommercial use), NASA POWER and NOAA NCEI. Other sources include USGS and AI-GPR. Data providers may delay, revise, block or restrict data; relays can see requests. Observation date is not necessarily publication date, so this is not a point-in-time backtest. Classifications and locations are broad proxies. Funds may contain the target. Not all supplied PDF formulas are implemented.
