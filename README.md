# SignalTrace — automatic combination research

## Install and use

Replace your GitHub repository's root `index.html` with the `index.html` from this folder, commit it, wait for GitHub Pages to redeploy, and refresh. The site is a single static file; it has not been uploaded to your repository automatically and does not require your own server or API key.

1. Enter a U.S. ticker or choose a company and press Analyze. The app loads a basic industry-selected set of daily data, plus regional weather candidates from Open-Meteo, NASA POWER and a NOAA weather station. Houston is the broad regional proxy; Chicago is used for applicable food, farm and transport classifications. These are not verified company locations.
2. To add a broader set, select **Load expanded data**. The progress panel counts completed scan jobs by category and elapsed time; a weather or geopolitical batch is one job but may contain many signals. The separate coverage panel counts individual signals that have finished loading, with loaded, unavailable and pending counts by category. Expand **Input coverage** to see names and substitutes. Unstarted jobs after Stop are not counted as unavailable. Two jobs run concurrently, with a short pause between jobs. **Stop** halts the queue after in-flight jobs finish and keeps the data already loaded. Provider failures are shown. This finite catalog is not an exhaustive list of all possible stock influences.
3. **Together**, **Earlier**, **Later** and **Explore** retain the line charts. The range dropdown and the **30 days** / **150 days** buttons control the displayed stock window. The 10-year option is available when the stock history supports it, making older events such as the 2020 COVID-19 milestones visible. Long windows may lack older data needed for capsule training.
4. **Combination research · automatic** runs in a background browser worker after data loads and again after the range or loaded inputs change. It tests individual signals and small equal-weight combinations, displays a progress bar, then shows three candidates selected on validation data with their separately calculated holdout results. Click **Show average lines** on a result to compare the visible stock line and the equal-weight input paths. Those lines are a visual guide, not the lagged-event test; the paths are unshifted, scaled/indexed and inverse inputs are flipped. Each result also has a date-aligned strip: gold numbered badges indicate stock capsules matched during holdout and coral marks indicate false alerts. A selected window may show only part of the holdout, so the visible numbers can be fewer than the full holdout hit count. The holdout can include dates earlier than the current display.
5. **All daily signals · capsule timelines** lists the stock and every successfully loaded daily signal in separately labeled rows. Search by name, or filter to tested signals or supported hypotheses. Green bars denote rising regimes; coral bars denote falling regimes; empty spaces can mean no regime or missing data. Some signals cannot produce capsules when their history or variability is inadequate. Hover a bar on desktop for dates, duration and excess area. Signals remain on their actual dates. **Compact: on** makes rows extra skinny; use the **+** on a row for its name and evidence, or turn compact mode off to show all details. Stock capsules are numbered in chronological order in the timeline and on the price chart. Numbers on source rows identify one-to-one matched stock confirmation events under that source’s trained lead and direction, not just overlapping bars. A stock capsule may have no matching input number. The numbering resets when the displayed date range changes; shorten a very long range if the price chart hides crowded labels.
6. The world-events strip and event list sit beside the capsule rows. Select a marker or an item in the event list for a description and source link. The recent-headlines list is separate; click an item to inspect it. Markers are placed at the next available stock session and retain the event's recorded date. Earthquake and news dates use UTC; they are not guaranteed to align with market hours. Event coverage is selective, not a complete world-event archive.
7. **Add songs** selects local audio files; they are not uploaded.

Annual migration and other annual World Bank indicators have been removed. Most event records are discrete contextual markers. One additional USGS input counts worldwide M6.5+ earthquakes in completed UTC calendar days between successive stock sessions; it excludes the current UTC day to avoid treating an unfinished day as complete. Each loaded daily numeric input has a capsule row.

## How the capsule and historical hypothesis screen works

This is a Seeq-inspired approximation, not the Seeq product or a full implementation of the supplied PDF. For the stock and each loaded daily input, the app computes a trailing five-trading-session sum of changes (stock/fund percentage returns; differences for non-price data). Each is standardized against an earlier history, requiring at least 40 valid pre-training observations. A rising/falling capsule begins after two consecutive standardized observations exceed +0.6 or fall below −0.6; the displayed start is backdated to the first of those two observations. It closes after two observations return inside the signed ±0.25 exit threshold, or at a gap or a maximum of 252 sessions. Excess area is the sum of standardized distance beyond the exit threshold, measured in standardized session units, not dollars.

For each daily source, when data allow:

1. Use up to 504 trading sessions **before** the validation interval to search lags of 1–10 sessions and both same- and inverse-direction relationships. Choose the lag and direction with the best training match rate minus its empirical baseline. At least eight training source events are required.
2. A source capsule's event time is its **second qualifying session**, the point at which the two-session entry can actually be confirmed. A match requires a stock capsule to confirm in the chosen direction within one session of the selected lag, always after the source. A stock event can be matched only once within a test window. Source events too close to the end for a complete response window and those with missing target data are excluded.
3. Report historical matches in the selected window and in the last 150 trading sessions ending at the same displayed end date. Both periods are after the training period, but they **overlap** each other—for example, the 30-session window is part of the 150-session window. The 30- and 150-day shortcuts use the same older training period and selected lag when their end date is the same.
4. The background baseline estimates how often a stock capsule of the relevant direction appeared within that lag band after any valid session, weighted by the source-event directions. The table also reports the mean and standard deviation of matched leads when enough background events are present. "Too few" indicates a small sample and does not qualify for a green label even if its raw percentage is high.
5. A green **Supported hypothesis** requires at least eight training and eight selected-window source events, at least twelve background source events, at least a 70% observed match rate in both selected and background windows, an older-training edge of at least five percentage points over its baseline, and a Wilson lower bound for the background match rate above the background baseline. These thresholds are pragmatic screens, not calibrated probabilities. The Wilson calculation assumes independent Bernoulli observations, an assumption that market events often violate.

A percentage is a **past matched-event fraction**, not a probability of causation, a buy signal, an expected investment return, or a chance of making money. The search tries many sources, lags and directions; there is no multiple-testing correction. Inputs can share a common cause or include the target stock itself. Similar capsules, even identical timing and duration, do not prove a causal relationship. News and macro data may be published later than their observation dates or revised; future information can therefore leak into an apparently historical test. Results are not point-in-time backtests, do not account for execution costs, and cannot establish profitability. Never use the green highlight alone to make a trade.

The original line-chart **Earlier** ranking is a separate daily-correlation screen with a 70% training / 30% holdout split within the displayed range; it can pick a different lag than the capsule screen. Later and Together are descriptive selected-window comparisons. Source lines are volatility-scaled, not raw prices; graph gaps are not invented data. The remaining PDF model code is only partially implemented and is not evidence of cause.

## Events and news

- The live [USGS FDSN earthquake catalog](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/) supplies global magnitude 6.5+ earthquakes for up to approximately ten years. Technology/communications stocks also receive a coarse Northern California bounding-box query for magnitude 4+ earthquakes. This does **not** verify that a company has a facility near any quake. Result limits and partial failures are reported. The derived count uses the completed UTC days from the previous stock-session date up to, but not including, the current stock-session date. Zero counts are used only when the global query succeeded without hitting its result cap. Magnitudes and the catalog can be revised.
- A small, fixed set of sourced reference milestones includes COVID-19's 2020 pandemic characterization, the first U.S. vaccine authorization, Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the 2023 France unrest, and the Israel–Gaza war escalation. These milestones do not constitute a comprehensive world history.
- The recent news section uses an industry-keyword [Bing News RSS](https://www.bing.com/news) search and [BBC Business RSS](https://feeds.bbci.co.uk/news/business/rss.xml). A simple keyword rule labels broad categories such as technology, conflict, energy and disasters. Headlines are not independently verified for relevance to the ticker, do not cover all news, and are not a complete historical archive. Click through to the publisher. The Bing and BBC headlines can change or fail; a public CORS relay can see the query.
- Expanded geopolitical context includes the newspaper-derived [AI-GPR Index](https://www.matteoiacoviello.com/ai_gpr.html) by Matteo Iacoviello and Jonathan Tong. It measures news-based risk, not every event or verified company exposure. Published series can be delayed or revised.

## Data sources, coverage and limits

The directory and current classification come from the third-party [top-us-stock-tickers Nasdaq-derived dataset](https://github.com/zyhe16/top-us-stock-tickers). Nasdaq Trader or Twelve Data may be used as a fallback directory without industry classifications. A curated sector and industry cross-reference selects the basic inputs; unknown classifications use broad-market signals. The expanded scan adds finite sets of sector funds, commodity futures, spot oil prices, bond/rate/currency series, geopolitical indices and daily weather observations. The expanded catalog includes 60 candidate market symbols and 32 candidate FRED series before removing already-loaded candidates, up to 16 AI-GPR series, 24 Open-Meteo regional weather series, 16 NASA weather series and 12 NOAA station series; actual availability varies. Some overlap already-loaded basic signals. NASA and Open-Meteo are retrospective gridded reanalyses; NOAA is a station observation. They may share underlying information and are not equivalent backups. It includes international and sector funds, commodity proxies, Bitcoin/Ethereum and additional Treasury, currency, credit and CBOE volatility series. Crypto daily charts use a 24-hour market and do not share U.S. equity close times. Target-stock volume and intraday high–low range are derived from that same stock and are not independent signals. Sector funds can hold the analyzed stock; futures and funds are proxies and not physical spot prices. None of the mappings verify a company's specific suppliers, customers, operating locations or causes.

Market prices and futures come from unofficial Yahoo endpoints; economic series from [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/), including the U.S. Economic Policy Uncertainty Index by Scott Baker, Nicholas Bloom and Steven J. Davis; volatility indices are from Cboe and may have additional copyright and usage restrictions. Selected failed basic FRED series may use explicitly labeled Yahoo proxies. Weather is [Open-Meteo historical reanalysis](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api) for Houston, Chicago, London, Singapore, Tokyo, Frankfurt, Doha and Rotterdam: mean temperature, precipitation and maximum wind in each region (up to 24 inputs from one batch). Data are CC BY 4.0 with Open-Meteo attribution; free API use is noncommercial. NASA POWER adds temperature, precipitation, mean wind and relative humidity for Houston, Chicago, London and Singapore. NOAA NCEI adds station maximum/minimum temperature, precipitation and average wind for Houston, Chicago and New York. These are different measures, observation locations, time conventions and methods, not three identical independent measurements. See [NASA POWER](https://power.larc.nasa.gov/) and [NOAA NCEI daily summaries](https://www.ncei.noaa.gov/products/land-based-station/global-historical-climatology-network-daily). NASA uses UTC days; station observation days depend on the station. Weather is retrospective and can be delayed. Public CORS relays cors.dev and Jina Reader may see requests, cache, fail, rate-limit or change. Yahoo data and other providers have terms and usage limits; check them before deployment. All provider data are retrieved live, not bundled. Some PDF formulas remain unimplemented.

## Accuracy and troubleshooting

The dollar-index Yahoo symbol `DX-Y.NYB` is kept intact; ordinary share-class symbols such as `BRK.B` are still converted to Yahoo’s `BRK-B` form. A previously missing feed may still fail if its provider, relay or rate limits are unavailable. An unavailable signal is not a zero; a loaded signal may nevertheless have gaps, stale data, insufficient prior history or no valid ranked comparison. FRED values can be assigned to observation dates before actual publication, weather is reanalysis, and earthquake data can be revised: none is a reliable point-in-time release calendar. No practical list here is exhaustive.

## Combination research: exactly what is tested

This is an exploratory comparison, not an exhaustive search, investment advice, proof of cause, or a calibrated probability. A web worker lets the browser remain responsive. It runs on the target and inputs available when the analysis starts; loading more data or changing the range reruns it. If the browser cannot create a worker or the history is insufficient, the other site features remain usable.

1. Use the same stock capsule confirmations as the numbered capsule timeline, with its threshold set using data before the capsule analysis window. For each input, take a trailing five-session sum of changes and use older training data to select one of ten leads and the direction with the largest absolute correlation to the stock's signed confirmation events. Each lead is at least one trading session. Standardization is fitted on that training period only; standardized extreme values are clipped to ±4.
2. Prescreen all inputs with sufficient aligned, varying training data. Compare the strongest **24 singles**, all **276 possible unordered pairs** among them, and the **56 unordered triples among the top eight**. Smaller sets produce fewer tests. This is at most 356 candidates, not every possible combination of all inputs, and it does not try factorial permutations or optimize arbitrary weights. A pair or triple is the equal-weight average of its members after each member's trained direction has been applied.
3. Split the analysis window chronologically: approximately 60% training, then 20% validation, then 20% holdout, with a ten-session gap before validation and another before holdout. Training picks the lag, sign and normalizing values; validation selects the top three; the final period is not used for that selection. It is a single split, not walk-forward or purged cross-validation. Date boundaries and sample counts are printed. Switching ranges or retrying repeatedly effectively reuses the holdout and weakens its independence.
4. The fixed alert threshold is +0.75 or −0.75 on the oriented average. An alert matches only when the stock has a confirmed capsule of the same direction on that alert's target session; each target session is counted at most once in that candidate. The components were observed at their trained earlier lags. Other alert sessions are false alerts, including an alert in the wrong direction. Eligible sessions require all components to be present. Candidates need at least 80% valid validation sessions and minimum event/alert counts; holdout sparsity is flagged instead of hidden. The hit rate is matched eligible stock events / eligible stock events; alert precision is matches / alerts. The direction-adjusted baseline is the holdout's signed event frequency weighted by the candidate's alert directions; it is descriptive, not a forecast.
5. The validation **score** is stock-event hit rate minus false alerts as a fraction of all eligible sessions. The same score is reported in holdout, but does not determine which three candidates appear. For pairs or triples, the displayed difference from the best member is calculated on the same eligible holdout sessions. No significance test or multiple-testing correction is applied; a positive difference can be random and is not a "smoking gun."

Because each selected signal is assigned its own lag, the numbers on these combination strips are **not** the same matching rule as the original individual capsule-row numbers. Both refer to the same numbered stock capsules; the combination strip evaluates lagged standardized changes on the exact stock confirmation date. If a stock capsule is missing a number on a result, that combination did not match it in the visible held-out region, or it lies outside the holdout. The indexed input-average chart stays on the original dates and is separate from the trained lagged event test. Do not interpret line proximity as held-out accuracy.

For a quick example, with nine eligible inputs the screen considers nine singles, 36 pairs, and 56 triples drawn from the top eight—not 9 factorial. Many predictors are highly correlated; sector funds may own the stock, and volume/range are derived from the target itself. News, macro observations, reanalyses and station archives may be revised or reported later than their observation dates; the app does not reconstruct when each was known. Consequently, even the holdout is not a point-in-time trading backtest and a good result may disappear in another period.

## Source redundancy

The three-weather-provider design increases resilience, but it does **not** provide three equivalent independent providers for every field, city, price, economic series or world-event index. If one weather provider fails, other successfully loaded observations remain available as separate labeled candidates; they are not silently substituted or merged into the failed series. NOAA's daily maxima/minima and average wind, for example, are not the same variables as the other providers' daily mean temperature and maximum wind. Availability is shown by provider and by individually checked signal; stopped, unstarted work is not counted as a failure. Yahoo market charts, FRED macro series, AI-GPR and USGS still have provider/relay concentration, and some labeled market proxies are only approximate substitutes. No free-source fallback is guaranteed.
